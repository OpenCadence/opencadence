# OpenCadence brand voice

This guide applies to user-facing copy across the product, website, documentation, release notes, and marketing materials.

## Our message

OpenCadence is an open-source workspace for freelancers and independent professionals. It brings clients, leads, projects, tasks, follow-ups, and notes into one calm, private place.

The central promise is simple: OpenCadence helps people understand what needs their attention and keep client work moving, while keeping them in control of their data.

## How we sound

### Clear

Use familiar language and short, direct sentences. Lead with what someone can do or why it matters. Explain specialist terms when they are necessary.

### Calm

Sound considered, dependable, and focused. Avoid urgency, hype, exaggerated claims, and excessive punctuation.

### Human

Write like a helpful person, not a corporate system. Use contractions where they make a sentence feel natural. Address the reader as “you” when useful.

### Honest

Describe the product as it works today. Be clear about limitations, privacy boundaries, and features that are only planned. Do not imply that local storage, self-hosting, or an AI connection provides guarantees it cannot make.

### Independent

Emphasise ownership, control, and freedom without criticising other products. OpenCadence should feel capable and reassuring, not rebellious or combative.

## Writing principles

- Start with the user benefit, then provide supporting detail.
- Prefer plain English over technical or business jargon.
- Keep paragraphs and interface messages concise.
- Use active voice and specific verbs.
- Use sentence case for headings and interface labels.
- Use a colon, comma, full stop, or parentheses instead of an em dash.
- Use lists when they make information easier to scan.
- Use “OpenCadence” as one word with this exact capitalisation.
- Use “OpenCadence Community” and “OpenCadence Cloud” for the editions.
- Use “clients” in user-facing copy. Use “customers” only when referring to an existing interface label or technical concept that uses that term.
- Use “AI assistant” in general copy. Introduce “MCP” only when the technical distinction is useful.
- Write website links as `opencadence.app` and link them to `https://opencadence.app`.

## Words to prefer

| Prefer | Avoid when possible |
| --- | --- |
| workspace | platform, ecosystem, solution |
| client work | workflow optimisation |
| keep work moving | maximise productivity |
| runs on your computer | local-first architecture |
| under your control | total control, complete security |
| connect an AI assistant | AI-powered, revolutionary AI |
| planned | coming soon, imminent |

## Examples

**Prefer:** See the tasks, follow-ups, and projects that need your attention today.

**Avoid:** Supercharge your productivity with a powerful, all-in-one workflow solution.

**Prefer:** Your Community workspace is stored on the computer running OpenCadence.

**Avoid:** Your data is completely private and secure.

**Prefer:** OpenCadence Cloud is planned for people who want managed hosting and connected services.

**Avoid:** OpenCadence Cloud is coming soon to transform how your team works.

## Final check

Before publishing user-facing copy, ask:

1. Does it quickly explain what OpenCadence helps someone do?
2. Is it understandable without technical knowledge?
3. Does it sound calm, useful, and honest?
4. Are current features clearly separated from planned features?
5. Does it avoid em dashes, hype, and unsupported claims?
